ISO 22301 certification in the UAE: how the audit actually runs

What certification checks, who may issue it, what the two stages look like from inside the room, what drives the price and the eight reasons organisations fail stage 2. How to build the system in the first place is covered separately.

What a certificate actually says

An ISO 22301 certificate states one narrow thing: an accredited independent body examined a defined scope of your organisation and found a business continuity management system that conforms to the standard and is in use. It does not say you will survive a crisis, and it does not say every part of the group is covered. The scope statement on the certificate is the part clients should read and rarely do.

Two distinctions save months. Certification is organisational, not personal: people take training and receive course certificates, entities get certified. And certification is not accreditation: the body that audits you is itself accredited by a national accreditation authority, and that accreditation is granted per standard. A body accredited for quality management is not thereby accredited for business continuity.

The two stages, from inside the room

  • Stage 1, documentation and readiness. Usually one to two days, often partly remote. The auditor reads the scope, policy, business impact analysis, risk assessment, strategies, plans, internal audit report and management review minutes. The output is a readiness opinion and a list of what must be in place before stage 2. A weak stage 1 delays rather than fails.
  • Stage 2, effectiveness on site. Two to five days depending on scope and sites. The auditor interviews activity owners, not the continuity manager: can the head of payments describe what happens in the first hour, and does that match the plan. Evidence is traced end to end, usually through one real incident and one exercise. Findings are raised as major or minor.
  • Closing the findings. Minor findings get a corrective action plan with dates. A major finding stops the certificate until it is closed and verified, sometimes with a return visit.
  • Surveillance. Annual visits on a sample of the system, then full recertification in the third year. The sample always includes exercises and corrective actions.

The single best predictor of a clean stage 2 is a recent exercise report that records what failed and what was fixed. An exercise where everything went well reads as theatre and invites harder questions.

What drives the price

Certification-body fees are quoted against headcount, number of sites and scope complexity, and they are usually the smaller part of the bill. The larger part is the time of your own activity owners during the BIA and the interviews. Four levers actually move the number.

LeverEffectHow to use it
Scope sizeEvery added entity and site multiplies interviews, plans and evidenceCertify the critical core first, extend at surveillance
Current maturityA recent BIA and a working recovery capability cut monthsRun a gap assessment before budgeting, not after
Multi-site samplingBodies may sample sites rather than visit all of themAsk how sampling is calculated before you compare quotes
Dual standardMapping to the national standard later means a second passDesign documents once with both standards mapped

Eight reasons organisations fail stage 2

  • Plans exist, but the people named in them have never read them.
  • The business impact analysis has no method, so recovery times cannot be defended.
  • Recovery objectives were set by IT and never agreed with the business that owns the service.
  • No exercise in the last twelve months, or an exercise with no findings.
  • Corrective actions from the last review are open and undated.
  • The internal audit was performed by the person who built the system.
  • Supplier dependencies are not assessed, although the service depends on them.
  • Management review happened, but no decisions and no resources are recorded.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO 22301 certification mandatory in the UAE?

No. It is voluntary. What makes it feel compulsory is procurement: tenders, parent companies and enterprise clients ask for the certificate as proof, and in regulated sectors it is strong evidence toward national expectations. Nothing in UAE law obliges a private company to hold it.

How long does certification take from a standing start?

About six months for a mid-size organisation to be certification-ready, then four to eight weeks for the two audit stages, depending on the body and the gap between them. Compressing below four months usually produces a paper system that stage 2 interviews expose.

What is the difference between stage 1 and stage 2?

Stage 1 checks that the management system exists on paper and that you are ready to be audited: scope, policy, BIA, risk assessment, plans, internal audit, management review. Stage 2 checks that it works: interviews with activity owners, evidence of exercises, corrective actions actually closed. Most failures happen at stage 2.

Who can issue the certificate?

An independent certification body, not a consultant. The same firm cannot both build your system and certify it: that conflict invalidates the certificate. Check that the body is accredited for ISO 22301 specifically, because accreditation is granted per standard.

How much does it cost?

Certification-body fees scale with headcount and number of sites, and they are the smaller half. The larger cost is internal effort, mostly the time of activity owners. Budget in the honest order: gap assessment first, then price the remediation from the register, then ask bodies for a quote.

Does ISO 22301 certification cover NCEMA 7000?

Not automatically, but the overlap is large because the national standard mirrors the structure of the international one. Documented once with both mapped, a single system satisfies both. Retrofitted afterwards, it means a second pass over every document.

What happens after the certificate is issued?

Surveillance audits, usually annual, and full recertification in the third year. The certificate lapses if the system stops running, so exercises, internal audits and management reviews have to keep happening on schedule.
