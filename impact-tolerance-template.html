Impact tolerance template: set, test and report

An impact tolerance says how much disruption to an important business service is too much. This page is the working part: how to fill in a tolerance for each service, test it and show the status to the board, with the Excel file to do it.

What goes into one row

The concept and the CBUAE rules behind it are explained on the CBUAE operational resilience page. Here, one row per important business service. Six fields are enough; the example is a retail card payment service.

FieldWhat to writeExample
Important business serviceA service customers or the market would miss, named from their sideRetail card payments
Who is harmedCustomers, counterparties, the marketRetail customers and merchants
Maximum disruptionHours or days beyond which harm becomes intolerable4 hours
Data lossHow much data may be lost30 minutes
Other metricVolume or value, where time alone misleadsNo more than 50,000 failed transactions
Mapped resourcesPeople, sites, technology and suppliers the service needsPayment switch, core banking, two data centres, processor

Tolerance, RTO and risk appetite are three different numbers

NumberPoint of viewWho sets itQuestion it answers
Impact toleranceCustomers and the marketBoardWhen does disruption cause intolerable harm?
RTOThe organisationService owner, from the BIABy when must we restore the service?
Risk appetiteThe organisationBoardHow much loss do we accept to pursue our goals?

The RTO sits inside the tolerance with a margin. If the RTO equals the tolerance, there is no room for the time it takes to notice the incident and decide. How the recovery numbers relate is shown on the recovery objectives page.

Setting a tolerance in five steps

  1. Name the service from the customer side. "Card payments", not "the payment switch".
  2. Describe the harm. Who is hurt and how the harm grows hour by hour.
  3. Pick the point where harm becomes intolerable. A maximum duration, plus a volume or data metric where duration alone misleads.
  4. Map the resources. People, sites, systems and suppliers, and which of them can break the tolerance on their own.
  5. Approve and review. The board approves the tolerances and reviews them at least once a year.

Testing against severe but plausible scenarios

For each service pick two or three scenarios: loss of a site, ransomware on a core system, failure of a key supplier. Run them as tabletop exercises or technical tests and record the worst disruption you could not avoid. Compare it with the tolerance. "Outside tolerance" is not a failed test: it is a vulnerability found in time, and it needs an owner and a date.

What the board sees

One line per service: the tolerance, the worst tested result, within or outside, and the remediation date. Above the lines, three counts: services within tolerance, outside tolerance and not yet tested. The template builds this view from the rows. The full structure of a board report is on the board reporting page.

Frequently asked questions

What is an impact tolerance?

The maximum tolerable level of disruption to an important business service, set from the point of view of customers and the market. It is expressed at least as a maximum duration, often with a volume or data loss metric.

How is an impact tolerance different from RTO?

The tolerance is the point of intolerable harm to customers and the market, approved by the board. The RTO is the internal target for restoring the service and must sit inside the tolerance with a margin.

Who approves impact tolerances?

The board, on the proposal of management, with a review at least once a year and after major changes to the business or a serious incident.

How do you report impact tolerance status to the board?

One line per important business service: tolerance, worst tested disruption, within or outside, remediation and date, with counts of services within, outside and not yet tested on top.
