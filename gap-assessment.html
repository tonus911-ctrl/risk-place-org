What it gives you

Five questions owners and boards bring to us, and the answer you get. A drone strike or ransomware readiness check covers one scenario in five weeks. This programme covers the whole company.

  • “What does one day of downtime cost us?”A figure in AED for every critical process: lost revenue, penalties, recovery costs. You can show it to the board and the insurer.
  • “Are we ready for a serious disruption?”We test it on two scenarios that are real for you, for example a ransomware attack and the loss of a site, and show where the plan breaks and how many days recovery takes.
  • “Who does what in the first hour?”Roles, authority and a first-hours checklist, rehearsed by your team on a tabletop day.
  • “How do we measure resilience?”Recovery time objectives (RTO) for key processes, acceptable data loss (RPO) and a readiness index you can track quarter by quarter, also on a dashboard.
  • “What do we show NCEMA 7000 assessors, the Central Bank or a large client?”Plans, test records and metrics as evidence, written in the terms of AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000 and ISO 22301.

How the work goes and when you get results

Three stages: understand, test, fix. Every step ends with a document you keep. A typical timeline for one company and two scenarios:

  1. Weeks 1–3Understand
    • Two scenarios chosen with you. By default, a ransomware attack and the loss of a site. For a trading house it may be a cornerstone contract, for a family business the departure of key executives, for a manufacturer a key supplier.
    • The economics of the company: where revenue comes from, how it turns into EBITDA, where clients, suppliers and single points of failure are concentrated.
    • Interviews with the people who make the money: who really holds each process together.
    First findings: weak points and the cost of a downtime day
  2. Weeks 4–5Test
    • A tabletop stress test: CRM or ERP goes dark, a warehouse or a production block is closed. Systems keep running as usual; the decisions, gaps and timings of the day are real.
    • The stress-test record: what broke, who decided what and how long recovery took.
    Gaps confirmed on your own processes
  3. Weeks 6–8Fix
    • The recovery scheme, built with your team: who does what, at which hour, with what authority. People carry out plans they helped to write.
    • Metrics and fixes: free fixes are made right away (plans, backup channels, contract wording, offline copies); measures that need a budget come with a payback case and a 90-day plan.
    Plans, roles, metrics and a 90-day plan

Time from your people: leadership joins the scenario session, the stress-test day and two reviews; key people give one interview of about an hour each. The analysis, modelling and documents are ours.

An example of one fix

The client register lives in the CRM, and that is exactly what ransomware encrypts first. During the programme we export it to a simple offline file kept away from the office and agree a backup channel: a direct contact for every key client, collected in advance, with message templates ready. Cost: zero. On the day of an incident, sales keep talking to clients while IT recovers. The programme is full of fixes like this.

Frequently asked questions

What does the quote include?

The quote is fixed for the agreed scope. Everything fixable without capital spend is fixed together during the programme: plans, registers, backup channels, contract wording, drills. Measures that need investment come with a risk-economics case that shows how they pay back; the decision stays yours.

How is this different from a scenario readiness check?

A readiness check covers one scenario, a drone strike or ransomware, in five weeks and ends with a report to the owner and a roadmap. The programme builds continuity for the whole company in 1–2 months: plans, roles, a stress test and metrics. 50% of the price of a check is credited if you move to the programme within six months.

Will you really switch off our systems?

No. The stress test is a tabletop exercise: we model the loss of CRM and ERP or the closure of a site in a structured exercise. Production is never touched, and the decisions, gaps and timings the day produces are real.

Our biggest risk is neither ransomware nor the loss of a site. Does the programme fit?

Yes. The two default scenarios are starting points. For a trading house it may be the failure of a cornerstone contract, for a family business the departure of key executives, for a manufacturer a key supplier. We agree on the two scenarios that really threaten you at the start.

Does the programme prepare us for NCEMA 7000 or the Central Bank?

It builds what an assessor looks for: a business impact analysis, plans, a tested recovery scheme and metrics, in the terms of AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000 and ISO 22301. Certification of a management system is done by an accredited body; we prepare the evidence.

What happens after the programme?

You keep the documents, the recovery metrics and the 90-day plan. Many clients continue with the continuity dashboard, the same metrics on one board-level screen, and an annual exercise. Both are optional.
