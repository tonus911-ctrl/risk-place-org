Disaster recovery for mission-critical systems in the UAE

A disaster recovery plan is judged on one day only: the day a mission-critical system stops. This page sets out what that plan must contain for an organisation in the UAE, from recovery objectives to the second site, backups, tests and where data may live, with a 30-point checklist to score your readiness.

What makes a system mission-critical

A system is mission-critical when its outage stops a critical service for longer than the business can tolerate. The list comes from the business impact analysis, not from the IT inventory: a modest file server can be mission-critical if payroll depends on it, and an expensive analytics platform may not be. Each mission-critical system needs a business owner as well as an IT owner.

Recovery objectives come from the business

RTO and RPO for a system are derived from the services that depend on it. The rule that catches most plans: the system RTO must be shorter than the RTO of every service that runs on it, because the service also needs time to restart and catch up. How the four recovery numbers relate is explained on the recovery objectives page.

A second site that does not fail with the first

  • A secondary site or cloud region for every mission-critical system, with capacity for the critical load.
  • No shared single point of failure. Check power, network carrier, cloud account and the identity service: a second data centre on the same carrier fails with the first.
  • Replication monitored against the RPO. Replication lag is an alert, not a monthly report.
  • Identity recoverable on its own. Without directory and multi-factor services, nobody can log in to the recovered systems.

Backups that survive ransomware

Ransomware targets backups first. At least one copy must be immutable or offline, encryption keys must be recoverable without the primary site, and each mission-critical system needs a restore test at least every six months. Keep configuration backups too: firewalls, network and cloud settings take longer to rebuild than servers.

Testing

Run a failover test of each mission-critical system at least once a year and a ransomware exercise that includes the decision to restore. Business users take part and confirm the service works; IT success without business sign-off is not a recovery. Every finding gets an owner and a date, and overdue actions go to management. Testing in the wider sense is covered on the operational resilience testing page.

Cloud, suppliers and where data may live

In the UAE, where data may be stored and moved depends on the sector and the type of data. The federal personal data protection law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, sets the general rules, and sector rules for banking, insurance and health data can be stricter. Check which rules apply before choosing a recovery site or cloud region outside the country, and write the data location and recovery commitments into hosting and cloud contracts. For organisations in scope of NCEMA 7000, disaster recovery evidence is part of what auditors ask for.

Frequently asked questions

What is a mission-critical system?

A system whose outage stops a critical service for longer than the business can tolerate. The list comes from the business impact analysis and each system has a business owner and an IT owner.

How far should a disaster recovery site be?

There is no single distance for every organisation. The site must be far enough that one event cannot hit both, and must not share power, network carrier or cloud account with the primary. Sector rules may add requirements.

How often should disaster recovery be tested?

A failover test of each mission-critical system at least once a year, and restore tests from backup at least every six months, with business users confirming the service works.

Can the disaster recovery site be in a cloud region outside the UAE?

It depends on the sector and the data. The federal data protection law and sector rules for banking, insurance and health data set where data may be stored. Check them before choosing the region and fix the location in the contract.
