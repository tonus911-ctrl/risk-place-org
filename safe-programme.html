Who it is for

Two different people, and the page speaks to one of them

The programme is taken by the crew. It is chosen and paid for by someone else, and that person has a different set of problems.

HSE managers

You have to show that induction happened, that it was understood, and that it covered what actually kills people on your sites. A signature sheet proves attendance, not understanding.

Contractors and subcontractors

The main contractor asks for evidence of safety training before mobilisation. You need something defensible, fast, and possible for a crew that arrived last week.

Manpower agencies

You place people on sites you do not control. A worker who arrives already trained, with a certificate that can be checked, is worth more and is placed faster.

The problem

Why imported safety training does not land here

Three things are true of a Gulf site and untrue of the courses usually bought for it.

01The crew does not read English well enough

A course delivered only in English is understood by the supervisor and skimmed by everybody else. What is not understood is not applied, and the record of completion says nothing about that.

02The heat is not a footnote here

Imported material treats heat as a paragraph about drinking water. In this region it is a seasonal occupational hazard with its own rules, its own emergency and its own way of killing people who feel fine an hour earlier.

03Turnover outruns the induction cycle

People arrive continuously and the annual training plan does not. A programme that needs a classroom, a trainer and a date will always be behind the actual composition of the crew.

SAFE was built against these three, in this order. Everything else on this page follows from them.

Three editions

The first four modules are common. The fifth is your industry.

The fifth module carries the scenarios, the barriers, the permit types and the shift checklist of one sector — because a warehouse checklist read on a rig is ignored by the second line.

Edition 01

Construction and civil works

Falls from height, struck-by, trench collapse, temporary electrical supplies. Scaffold tags, exclusion zones under lifts, shoring regardless of how short the job is.

Edition 02

Oil, gas and petrochemicals

Loss of containment, fire and explosion, confined space, hydrogen sulphide. Isolation, gas testing, standby arrangements and the discipline around the permit.

Edition 03

Logistics and warehousing

Forklift and pedestrian separation, racking collapse, loading dock falls. Blind spots, height lines, wheel chocks and the rule against climbing racking.

One organisation can run more than one edition side by side — the certificate states which one was taken.

The part nobody else has

Heat, treated as the occupational hazard it is

Every summer the same thing happens on Gulf sites, and every year the training bought for it says almost nothing about it. SAFE gives heat a full chapter in the hazards module and a second one in the response module.

What the crew learns

That heat takes judgement before it takes strength — a man becomes slower and less accurate an hour before he feels unwell, and he does not notice it in himself. That thirst arrives late, so drinking starts before it. That acclimatisation takes about two weeks and the newly arrived worker is the highest risk on the site. And that a colleague who has stopped sweating is not recovering.

What the supervisor gets

The midday break calendar for the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar with the actual dates and hours, the employer's obligations on shade, cooling, drinking water and salt replacement, and the distinction that decides a life: cool damp skin with a clear head is exhaustion; hot dry skin with confusion is heat stroke, and that is an emergency call, not a rest in the shade.

The exempted works — asphalt, concrete pours, emergency repairs — are usually the hottest of all. That is exactly why the rest of the protections matter more there, not less.

Two languages

The worker reads it in his language. You get the record in yours.

The Arabic edition is written, not machine-translated. Regulations carry their official Arabic names; designations such as ISO 45001 and OSHAD-SF stay as they are written in Arabic documents, so a supervisor can still find them.

What that changes on site

Comprehension stops depending on the supervisor's willingness to translate. A crew of mixed nationalities gets the same content rather than a summary passed down the line. And the chapters that matter most — the right to stop work, the heat emergency, the 48-hour rule — are the ones most often lost in an informal translation.

What stays the same

The diagrams are identical in both editions, so a supervisor pointing at a figure is pointing at the same thing the worker sees. Completion records, test scores and certificates come to you in one consistent format regardless of which language the person studied in.

Assessment

How you know it was understood, not just opened

Two levels, and both of them resist the two things people do with online safety training: clicking through, and passing the answers around.

Level 1

Module checks

Each module ends in a short check on its own material. It opens only once every chapter of that module has been marked as read.

Level 2

Final assessment

Fifteen situations across all five modules. Twelve correct to pass. It opens only after every module and every module check is complete.

Design

Shuffled every attempt

Both the questions and the answer options are reordered on each attempt, so neither the sequence nor the position of the correct answer can be memorised or passed on.

Design

Situations, not definitions

No question asks what a term means. Every one describes something happening on a shift and asks what to do about it.

Attempts are unlimited. The point is that the crew ends up knowing this, not that somebody fails.

The certificate

Named, dated, and checkable by whoever asks

Issued on completion of the final assessment. It carries the person's name, the industry edition taken, the date, and a number that can be verified.

What it is

Evidence that a named individual worked through this programme and passed an assessment on it. Useful to a main contractor asking what your crew has been through, and to a worker moving between employers with a record that travels with him.

What it is not

It is not a state accreditation and does not replace any statutory qualification your jurisdiction requires. We say this plainly on the certificate itself, because a document that overclaims is worse than none — it fails at the moment it is checked.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take?

Around four hours of reading and answering. Most organisations run it across one shift, or split it over two or three breaks. Progress is saved, so stopping costs nothing.

Do the workers need accounts or email addresses?

No. The person opens a link, enters their name once for the certificate, and starts. Many site workers have no work email, and a programme that requires one does not get completed.

Does it work without a good connection?

The page loads once and then runs from the phone. A weak signal on site does not interrupt someone halfway through a module.

Can we host it ourselves?

Yes. It is a single file with nothing external in it. Put it on your intranet or your own server and it behaves identically.

Is the Arabic a translation of the English?

It is a separate edition, written rather than translated, with regulations under their official Arabic names. The diagrams are common to both so that a supervisor and a worker are looking at the same figure.

Does this satisfy our statutory training obligation?

Not by itself, and we will not claim otherwise. Where your jurisdiction requires certified training by an approved provider, this programme supports that obligation rather than replacing it. What it does give you is evidence that the content was worked through and assessed.

Can the content be adapted to our site?

Yes — your rules, your permit forms, your emergency numbers and your muster points can be built into the fifth module. That is a separate piece of work and is quoted on its own.

What happens when the regulations change?

The seasonal figures and the regulatory references are reviewed annually. Existing clients get the updated file.
