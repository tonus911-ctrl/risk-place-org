How the practice works

Three things define how we work with a client and a student.

We came to resilience from inside organisations.

The plans we write for clients we first executed ourselves — in an industrial giant, in an international bank, under a regulator's inspection. A person who has lived through a real incident knows that in the first hour nobody opens a 300-page document. So our plans are short and written for the worst day.

We count risk in money.

A conversation with the board about "raising resilience" ends with nothing; a conversation about the cost of a downtime day ends with a decision. In every engagement a model appears where a disruption reaches revenue and profit, and every day of recovery has a price.

We build the system together with the client's team.

A plan people see for the first time on the day they need it stays on paper. We assemble the recovery scheme in working sessions with those who will act, and leave the organisation a shared language along with the documents.

The practice covers industry, finance, insurance, transport, IT and retail; ISO 22301 and AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000; preparing organisations for audits and for the conversation with the regulator. More than eight hundred professionals have completed our programmes. The methodology is published openly — in the library and the glossary on this site.

What we stand on

Open knowledge.

The library — 215+ pages and the only specialised Arabic-English glossary online — is free to read without registration. We earn on certification, tools and consulting.

Measurable resilience.

A continuity programme proves itself with a number the board can read: the cost of a downtime day, the recovery target against reality. The dashboard and the audit exist to produce those numbers.

The Gulf in its own language.

NCEMA, SAMA, the central banks and the financial centres have built serious requirements. ERGP is the first resilience governance certification fully available in Arabic, written for that regulatory layer.
