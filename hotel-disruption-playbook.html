Hotel business continuity in a disruption: guests, water, food, claims and staff

A power cut, a water outage or an incident nearby stops a hotel at full occupancy within hours. This guide shows the five decisions that decide the loss, and what to have ready before the day comes.

Five decisions that decide the loss

The questions general managers bring to us on the first day of a disruption, and the answer that keeps guests and money.

  • “Where do our guests sleep tonight?”Move guests to the wing that still has power and water, close the rest, and have partner hotels ready for the overflow.
  • “Is there water for everyone?”A reserve counted in litres per person per day, bottled drinking water on top, and a signed tanker contract.
  • “What do we serve tomorrow?”A short menu built from 72 hours of dry and frozen stock, and a second supplier for every critical item.
  • “How do we avoid claims?”Early written notice, honest options, no charges for services that are closed, and a log of every decision.
  • “What do we do with staff?”A core crew in two shifts, the rest on agreed leave or back-office work, and pay decisions taken before layoffs.

Guests: who stays, who moves, who leaves

Three options for every guest, decided in the first hour and written down.

  • Consolidate.Move in-house guests to the wing with power and water, close the upper floors to save lifts and pumps, and keep one floor free for families and guests with medical needs.
  • Relocate.Agree in advance with two or three partner hotels within 30 minutes: rate, number of rooms, transport. A signed one-page agreement turns a crisis call into a booking.
  • Help them leave.Offer early departure without penalty, help with flights and transfers, and refund unused nights in one step.
  • Know who needs help first.Export a nightly list from the property system: elderly guests, guests with disabilities, families with infants, medical needs. If the system is down, the paper list still works.

Water: a reserve counted per person, not per tank

Food suppliers: a short menu and a second source

Food is a cost line that turns into a loss line within a day when deliveries stop.

  • 72 hours of stock for a short menu.Dry and frozen stock for three hot dishes, breakfast and room service. A set menu cuts purchases and waste.
  • Two suppliers for every critical item.Water, bread, dairy, fresh produce, gas and diesel. The second supplier must use a different route or warehouse.
  • A priority clause in contracts.Delivery priority and an agreed price in a disruption, written in advance. Without it the hotel pays the spot price.
  • Cold chain discipline.Log temperatures, follow your food safety plan for discard limits, and record what was thrown away and why: the insurer will ask.

Claims: how a disruption turns into a dispute, and how to avoid it

Most complaints and claims after a disruption come from four things: guests were not told in time, they were charged for services that were closed, safety was not recorded, or the desk promised what the hotel could not deliver.

  • Written notice within the first hour.What happened, what works, what does not, and the guest’s options. In every room, by message and at the desk, in Arabic and English.
  • Honest options and no charges for closed services.Adjust the rate or credit the pool, spa and restaurant while they are closed. Check changes to the cancellation policy with your lawyer.
  • A decision log.Time, decision, who took it, what guests were told. Photos of conditions and safety checks. This is the evidence for the insurer, tour operators and any dispute.
  • Contracts before the day.Check force majeure wording with tour operators, booking platforms and event clients, and the insurer’s notification deadline.

Staff: a core crew, not a full house, and no panic layoffs

Payroll keeps running when revenue stops. The aim is to cut cost without losing the people you need when guests return.

Source: The National, 1 March 2026

  • A core crew in two shifts.Front office, security, engineering, core housekeeping and kitchen. Twelve-hour shifts with rest, meals and accommodation on site.
  • Back office from home.Sales, finance and reservations work remotely. In March 2026 the UAE asked private companies to switch to remote work where possible.
  • Leave and hours by agreement first.Annual leave, reduced hours and rotation agreed with staff before any layoff. Rehiring and retraining cost more than a few weeks of pay.
  • Cross-training before the day.Two people trained for every critical role: generator start, water checks, the front desk system offline.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should a hotel keep for an outage?

Count people, not rooms: guests and staff on site times 15 litres per person per day, the humanitarian minimum, for the days you plan to hold out. Keep bottled drinking water on top and a signed tanker contract.

Is it better to keep guests or move them to another hotel?

Keep them if you can give power, water and safety in one wing. Move them when you cannot, and agree partner hotels in advance so the move takes an hour, not a day.

Can we charge the full rate if the pool, spa or restaurant is closed?

Charging for services that were not delivered is the most common trigger of complaints and disputes. Adjust the rate or give a credit while they are closed, and check changes to your terms with a lawyer.

What should we do with staff if occupancy falls for weeks?

Keep a core crew, move the back office to remote work, and agree leave, reduced hours and rotation before any layoff. People you lose now are the hardest cost to recover.

Where do we start?

With three documents this week: the offline list of guests who need help first, the water reserve count and the supplier matrix. Then test them in a one-hour tabletop exercise.
