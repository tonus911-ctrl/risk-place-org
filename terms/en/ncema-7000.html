AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000

معيار استمرارية الأعمال الإماراتي · also written NCEMA 7000
AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000 is the national business continuity standard of the United Arab Emirates, issued by NCEMA and mandatory for government entities and critical infrastructure.

The full designation reads AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000:2021. AE is the country code for the United Arab Emirates, SCNS is the Supreme Council for National Security, NCEMA is the National Emergency Crisis and Disasters Management Authority, and 7000 is the number of the standard. In everyday use it is shortened to NCEMA 7000, which is why both spellings appear across documents, tenders and job descriptions.

The standard sets out how an organisation establishes, runs and improves a business continuity management system: understanding what the organisation must keep delivering, analysing impact, choosing recovery strategies, writing plans, exercising them and proving that the people responsible are competent. The management system logic will look familiar to anyone who has worked with ISO 22301. What NCEMA 7000 adds is national: alignment with the UAE emergency framework, notification duties towards the authorities, and a defined place in national crisis coordination.

Compliance is mandatory for federal and local government entities and for organisations designated as critical infrastructure. Private companies are pulled in a different way, through contracts. A client under the standard has to be able to evidence the continuity of its supply chain, so the requirement travels down to suppliers who were never named in any regulation. For many organisations the first encounter with NCEMA 7000 is a clause in a tender document rather than a letter from a regulator.

What the standard asks for, in practice

  1. Scope and context. Which entities, sites and services are covered, and which external obligations apply.
  2. Business impact analysis. Which activities matter, how quickly harm accumulates, and what the minimum acceptable level of service is.
  3. Recovery strategies. How each critical activity will be kept running or restored, and what resources that requires.
  4. Plans and procedures. Documented, current and usable by the people who will actually open them at three in the morning.
  5. Exercising and testing. Evidence that the plans were rehearsed, and a record of what the rehearsal changed.
  6. Competence. Named people, defined roles, and training records that show they can perform them.
  7. Review and improvement. Management review, corrective actions and a trail showing the system moves.

NCEMA 7000 and ISO 22301 compared

AspectAE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000ISO 22301
StatusNational standard, mandatory for defined entitiesInternational standard, voluntary certification
AuthorityNCEMA, under the Supreme Council for National SecurityISO, with accredited certification bodies
National coordinationExplicit duties towards the UAE emergency frameworkNot addressed
AudienceUAE government, critical infrastructure and their suppliersAny organisation, any country

The two are not rivals. An organisation already running an ISO 22301 system has most of the machinery in place and needs to add the national layer. An organisation starting from NCEMA 7000 can later certify to ISO 22301 with limited extra work. Which to take first is decided by who is asking: the regulator, or the international client.

Common misunderstandings

  • Treating it as a document set. Auditors look for evidence that plans were exercised and improved, not for a folder of files.
  • Assuming it applies only to government. Supply-chain clauses reach far beyond the entities named in the regulation.
  • Skipping competence. Training records are part of the evidence, not an optional courtesy. See AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000 training and courses.
  • Copying an ISO 22301 manual unchanged. The national requirements are exactly the part that will be checked and exactly the part a copied manual omits.

Questions

What does AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000 stand for? AE is the country code for the United Arab Emirates, SCNS is the Supreme Council for National Security, NCEMA is the National Emergency Crisis and Disasters Management Authority, and 7000 is the standard number. It is commonly shortened to NCEMA 7000.

Who must comply with NCEMA 7000? Federal and local government entities and organisations designated as critical infrastructure. Private companies are increasingly pulled in through contracts, because their clients pass the requirement down the supply chain.

How is NCEMA 7000 different from ISO 22301? The management system logic is close, but NCEMA 7000 adds national requirements: alignment with the UAE emergency framework, notification of the authorities, and a defined role in national crisis coordination.

Is NCEMA 7000 training required? The standard requires demonstrable competence of the people who run continuity. Training is the usual way to evidence it, together with an assessment and a dated record an auditor can open.

Which edition is current? AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000:2021 is the edition organisations are audited against.
