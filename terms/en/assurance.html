Assurance

التأكيد
Independent confirmation to the board that resilience actually works: evidence, testing and audit rather than management's own word.

Assurance exists because boards cannot govern on management's self-assessment: the people who built the capability are the least neutral witnesses to whether it works. Independent assurance through internal audit, external review and testing gives the board evidence rather than reassurance. This changes the board conversation from what management says about readiness to what the tests and audits actually show. For directors personally, assurance is protection, because it demonstrates that oversight was exercised on facts, not on impressions.

In practice, assurance draws on the three-lines model: management owns controls, risk and compliance functions monitor, and internal audit provides the independent view to the audit committee and board. Regulators lean on the same machinery: CBUAE expects banks to evidence their resilience, implementation of NCEMA 7000 in UAE government and critical-infrastructure entities is subject to review rather than self-declaration, and ISO 22301 certification adds an external audit layer for those who adopt it. Assurance findings are fixed in audit reports, test results and board minutes. Compliance and assurance are linked, because the same tested facts that prove conformity to a regulator prove capability to the board.

The recurring failure is assurance reduced to document-checking: audits confirm that plans exist, are dated and signed, and say nothing about whether recovery would actually happen. Paper assurance is more dangerous than none, because it manufactures confidence without capability. Real assurance follows the evidence chain to exercises, incidents and closed findings. Building an assurance picture a board can rely on is the subject of ERGP module M6, Assurance, reporting and maturity.
